# Agent 1: Bank Blackout Windows Research

## Bank Buyback Blackout Policies and Stress Test Analysis

### 1. Additional Blackout Periods Beyond Standard Earnings Blackouts

#### Federal Reserve Stress Test / CCAR Periods

**Yes, banks have additional blackout restrictions around the Fed stress test cycle:**

- **Capital Plan Submission Period**: Banks submit their capital plans (FR Y-14A) typically in April. During the second quarter (April-June), the Federal Reserve conducts its CCAR review.

- **CCAR Blackout Period**: The Federal Reserve established a one-quarter "blackout period" during CCAR (Q2 of each calendar year). During this time, bank holding companies cannot submit notices to use the de minimis exception or request prior approval for additional capital distributions.

- **Post-Results Quiet Period**: Banks must wait until after 4:30 p.m. EDT on the day following stress test results release (typically early July) before publicly disclosing capital actions (dividend increases, buyback programs).

- **SCB Uncertainty Period**: Between stress test results (late June) and final Stress Capital Buffer (SCB) requirements being announced (by August 31), banks operate with preliminary requirements. Final capital requirements become effective October 1 (though the Fed has proposed shifting this to January 1).

#### Regulatory Consequences

If a bank falls below minimum risk-based requirements in the stress test, it remains subject to additional capital distribution restrictions for three extra months (through September 30).

### 3. Typical Earnings Blackout Window for Banks

**Standard practice: 2-5 weeks before earnings through 48 hours after release**

| Source | Blackout Start | Blackout End |

**Important caveat**: There is no federally-mandated buyback blackout. These are voluntary company policies to avoid insider trading concerns under SEC Rule 10b-5.

### 4. 10b5-1 Pre-Programmed Plans vs. Discretionary Purchases

#### Major Banks Use Both Approaches

**10b5-1 Plans:**
- Allow buybacks during blackout periods
- Must specify price ranges, volume, and timing in advance
- Provide legal "safe harbor" against insider trading claims
- Associated with higher completion rates and faster execution
- JPMorgan has historically used 10b5-1 plans to maintain steady repurchases (e.g., 16 million shares/month in Q3 2017)

**Discretionary (Open Market) Purchases:**
- Governed by SEC Rule 10b-18 safe harbor
- More flexibility on timing and volume
- Cannot execute during earnings blackouts
- Subject to daily volume limits (25% of average daily volume)

**Key Distinction:**
- Rule 10b5-1 = Shield against insider trading claims
- Rule 10b-18 = Shield against market manipulation claims

Banks typically use a combination: 10b5-1 plans for baseline repurchases, with discretionary authority for opportunistic buybacks during open windows.

# Agent 3: 10b5-1 Plans Research

## Rule 10b5-1 Plans and Corporate Buybacks During Blackout Periods

### 1. Percentage of Corporate Buybacks via 10b5-1 vs. Discretionary Open-Market Purchases

**Key Finding: Approximately 25-40% of buybacks involve Rule 10b5-1 plans, with ~60% of programs able to continue during blackouts.**

The most authoritative academic research comes from Bonaimé, Harford, and Moore's study "Payout Policy Trade-Offs and the Rise of 10b5-1 Preset Repurchase Plans" (Management Science, 2020):

- **Approximately one quarter (25%) of all repurchase announcements include a Rule 10b5-1 component** (based on data from 2011-2014)
- Rule 10b5-1 plans became more than **twice as popular as accelerated share repurchases (ASRs)**
- The use of 10b5-1 for buybacks grew from only 4 announcements in 2001 to over **200 announcements per year** during 2011-2014

JPMorgan analysts have estimated that **approximately 60% of buyback programs are not affected by blackout periods** because they operate under 10b5-1 plans or similar arrangements.

### 2. Do 10b5-1 Buybacks Continue at the Same Pace During Blackout Periods?

**Key Finding: Yes, 10b5-1 buybacks can and do continue during blackout periods at pre-determined rates.**

Rule 10b5-1 permits trading during blackout periods provided companies have set up a plan to buy back stock on a regular, defined basis with specified price parameters (minimum and maximum) and share quantities.

- Companies executing buybacks under 10b5-1 plans have an affirmative defense against insider trading accusations
- The plans run on "autopilot" with predetermined price, volume, and timing parameters

**Evidence of continued activity:**
- JPMorgan, which reported earnings on January 14, bought back **14.1 million shares in January** (the month containing their earnings release), compared to 8.0 million in February and 6.9 million in March - suggesting they continued buybacks through their blackout period using a 10b5-1 plan

However, research notes that **repurchase volume increases by 42% on average from the first to the second month of a fiscal quarter** because earnings announcements typically occur early in the second month and many firms treat the period beforehand as a blackout.

### 3. Major Companies Known to Use 10b5-1 for Buybacks

#### Apple
According to their SEC 10-K filing:
> "The programs do not obligate the Company to acquire a minimum amount of shares. Under the programs, shares may be repurchased in privately negotiated or open market transactions, **including under plans complying with Rule 10b5-1** under the Exchange Act."

Apple has authorized massive buyback programs:
- $110 billion program announced May 2024 ($69.2 billion utilized by March 2025)
- Additional $100 billion program announced May 2025
- Apple was responsible for **more than 10% of all stock buybacks** in 2024

#### Goldman Sachs
Has indicated their purchases "shall be in accordance with Rules 10b5-1 and 10b-18 under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934."

#### JPMorgan Chase
Authorized a $50 billion share repurchase plan (replacing a previous $30 billion authorization). Their buyback patterns suggest use of 10b5-1 plans given continued activity during blackout periods.

### 4. Companies Known to Use ONLY Discretionary Buybacks (No 10b5-1)

**Key Finding: Berkshire Hathaway is the most prominent example of a purely discretionary buyback approach.**

**Berkshire Hathaway** under Warren Buffett operates a purely discretionary buyback program:

> "Berkshire's common stock repurchase program permits Berkshire to repurchase its shares any time that Warren Buffett believes that the repurchase price is below Berkshire's intrinsic value, conservatively determined."

Key characteristics of Berkshire's approach:
- No pre-set 10b5-1 plan
- Buffett makes discretionary decisions based on intrinsic value assessment
- Must maintain at least $30 billion in cash/equivalents before any repurchase
- Has gone **12 consecutive months** (as of 2025) without any buybacks when valuation is not attractive

### 5. SEC's December 2022 10b5-1 Rule Changes and Impact on Blackout Buybacks

**Key Finding: The SEC exempted corporate buyback programs from the most restrictive new requirements.**

#### What Changed for Individuals (Directors/Officers):
- **90-120 day cooling-off period** before first trade under a new plan
- Certification requirement that they are unaware of MNPI
- Ban on overlapping plans
- Restrictions on single-trade plans

#### What Did NOT Apply to Corporate Buybacks:
- **No cooling-off period required** for issuer buyback programs
- Issuers may still use overlapping 10b5-1 repurchase plans
- Issuers may still enter into ASRs and structured repurchases under 10b5-1

### 6. How Much Does 10b5-1 Usage Reduce the "Blackout Effect" on Stock Prices?

**Key Finding: Research shows minimal to no statistically significant "blackout effect" on stock prices, largely due to 10b5-1 plan usage.**

#### Research Evidence:

**State Street Global Advisors (SSGA)** research found:
- "No significant difference in returns in any of the periods measured of heavy buyback program firms"
- "No strong linkage between buybacks and any performance degradation during blackout periods"

**O'Shaughnessy Asset Management** research shows:
- Annualized excess returns during **open windows: +3.3%**
- Annualized excess returns during **blackout windows: +3.2%**
- Conclusion: "Excess returns from stocks that are repurchasing are driven not from the stock repurchases, but rather from the information coming from earnings reports"

**JPMorgan's Perspective:**
> "Keep in mind that blackout periods only affect discretionary buyback programs. We estimate that ~60% of buyback programs are not affected by the blackout. While a blackout period is not positive for equities, its impact should not dominate the price action of the market."

#### Quantitative Impact:
- The **0.85 correlation** between 4-week buyback changes and stock market movements suggests a relationship
- However, during blackouts, approximately **60% of buyback programs continue** via 10b5-1 plans
- Companies with higher buyback ratios may see corporate demand drop by approximately **35-40%** during blackouts (the discretionary portion), not 100%

# Agent 4: Buyback Impact Academic Studies

## Research on Buyback Blackout Periods and Stock Price Effects

### 1. Relationship Between Buyback Intensity and Blackout Period Price Effects

**Key Research Finding: Limited to No Significant Negative Effect**

The most rigorous study on this topic is from **SPDR Americas Research (State Street Global Advisors)** by Matthew J. Bartolini and Aram Kaplanian titled "Buyback Blackout Periods Do Not Negatively Impact Performance". Their findings:

- Out of 136 months analyzed, it was **nearly an even split** between positive and negative excess returns for the S&P 500 Buyback Index vs. S&P 500 Equal Weighted Index during blackout periods
- The residual alpha during blackout periods was "not significant" - there was actually **positive alpha generated, albeit insignificant**
- Firms with heavy buyback programs actually **outperformed** during blackout periods

**O'Shaughnessy Asset Management research** found:
- Annualized excess returns for top-decile buyback stocks in the **open window: +3.3%**
- Annualized excess returns for top-decile buyback stocks in the **blackout window: +3.2%**
- Conclusion: Excess returns are driven by **underlying economic value generation**, not the buyback activity itself

### 2. Evidence of Stock Price Weakness When Corporate Buybacks Pause

**Mixed Evidence - Correlation vs. Causation Debate**

Some analysts argue:
- A **0.85 correlation** exists between 4-week percentage change in buybacks vs. stock market
- When the blackout period begins, corporate demand (the largest buyers of equities) drops by **~35%**

However, SPDR's research counters this:
- When normalizing buyback amounts by market capitalization, the correlation drops from **77% to just 5%**
- This refutes the argument that buybacks are "the only thing keeping the stock market afloat"

**JP Morgan's Marko Kolanovic** noted:
- Blackout periods only affect **discretionary buyback programs**
- Approximately **60% of buyback programs are NOT affected** by blackouts
- While a blackout period is "not positive for equities, its impact should not dominate price action"

### 3. Studies on "Buyback Blackout Periods" and Stock Performance

**Academic Research on Corporate Calendar Effects**

The **ECGI Working Paper** by Ingolf Dittmann (Harvard Law School) found:
- From the first to the second month of a fiscal quarter, repurchase volume increases by **42% on average**
- Typical blackout period: **5 weeks before earnings to 2 days after**
- Academic research (Bettis et al., 2000) found **78.11% of firms have blackout periods**
- Jagolinzer et al. (2011) found the average blackout period includes **46 days before and 1 day after** earnings

**Quantpedia research** on buyback timing and earnings:
- Managers choose to buyback stocks when they expect **good earnings announcements**
- The market reaction to earnings following repurchase announcements is **4.56% higher** than earnings following secondary equity offerings (SEOs) over a 25-day period

### 4. Buyback as Percentage of Daily Volume Affecting Price Impact

**Regulatory Framework: SEC Rule 10b-18**

- SEC Rule 10b-18 limits issuers to purchasing no more than **25% of average daily volume**
- This safe harbor prevents liability for market manipulation
- On September 18, 2008 (post-Lehman), SEC raised this limit to **100%**, leading to an **84% increase** in buyback announcements

**The Gabaix-Koijen "Inelastic Markets Hypothesis"** (NBER Working Paper):
- **Groundbreaking finding**: Every $1 invested in the stock market increases market value by approximately **$5** (multiplier range: 3-8)
- Stock market inelasticity means buybacks have a **persistent material impact** on aggregate market volatility and growth
- Price impact is "quite long-lasting" - not just because of information release, but because permanent demand shifts create permanent price shifts

**Practical Price Impact Research** (McKinsey):
- Small repurchase programs see average share price increase of **2-3%** on announcement day
- Large buybacks (15%+ of shares) see prices increase by **~16%** on average
- Stock buybacks at ~$1.5 trillion/year = ~$6 billion daily buying = **~0.5% of daily equity trading volume**

### 5. Cases Where Large Buyback Programs Showed NO Blackout Effect

**Apple Case Study**:
- Apple uses **Rule 10b5-1 plans** allowing automated buybacks even during blackout periods
- From April 1 to May 5, Apple bought 67,707 shares at average price of $170.46 - **immediately before reporting earnings on May 1**
- Apple strategically bought more when stock price was lower, less when higher
- About **80% of companies** have programs set up for automatic buybacks during blackout periods

**Broader Market Evidence**:
- Finom Group calls the blackout period effect a "myth"
- The study by Bartolini and Kaplanian found "no strong linkage between buybacks and market declines during blackout periods"
- Firms with high buyback ratios show "mostly idiosyncratic performance" independent of blackout timing

### Conclusion

The weight of academic and analyst research suggests that:

1. **Buyback blackout periods do NOT cause significant negative market performance** - the effect is largely a myth
2. **The correlation between buybacks and market performance** is substantially reduced when normalized for market capitalization
3. **Rule 10b5-1 plans** allow approximately 60-80% of buyback activity to continue during blackouts
4. **Buyback intensity affects price impact** through the Gabaix-Koijen multiplier effect (~5x), but this operates independently of blackout timing
5. **Large programs like Apple's** demonstrate that sophisticated companies circumvent blackout limitations entirely

The primary drivers of stock returns for high-buyback companies appear to be **underlying fundamentals and earnings quality**, not the mechanical presence or absence of buyback activity during specific calendar windows.

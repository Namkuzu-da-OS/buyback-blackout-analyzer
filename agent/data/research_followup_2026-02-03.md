# Follow-up Research Findings - February 3, 2026

## CRITICAL FINDING: The Blackout Effect May Be Weaker Than We Thought

Academic research suggests the "buyback blackout effect" is **largely a myth** for most stocks:

| Source | Finding |
|--------|---------|
| **State Street (SPDR)** | "No significant negative impact" during blackouts |
| **O'Shaughnessy** | Open window returns: +3.3%, Blackout returns: +3.2% (nearly identical) |
| **JP Morgan** | ~60% of buyback programs continue during blackouts via 10b5-1 |
| **Finom Group** | Calls the blackout effect a "myth" |

**Why?** Most large companies use **Rule 10b5-1 plans** that allow automated buybacks to continue during blackout periods. Apple, Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan all do this.

### Implications for Our Strategy

Our Apple analysis showed a clear pattern. But the broader research says this shouldn't exist. Possible explanations:

1. **Apple is unique** - Maybe the pattern only works for Apple specifically
2. **January is unique** - Holiday liquidity + year-end dynamics may amplify the effect
3. **Intra-blackout timing matters** - Research looked at entire blackout, not early/mid/late phases
4. **Regime effects** - When stock enters blackout weak, the effect is amplified

**We need to validate:** Does our specific approach (late blackout entry, 15-day post-earnings exit) work on other stocks, or is Apple an anomaly?

## Bank-Specific Findings

### Banks Have ADDITIONAL Blackout Windows

| Period | Window | Notes |
|--------|--------|-------|
| **Q1 Earnings** | Late March - Mid-April | Standard earnings blackout |
| **CCAR Submission** | April | Capital plan review |
| **Stress Test Review** | May - June | Q2 is restricted |
| **Results Quiet Period** | Late June - Early July | Cannot announce until after 4:30 PM day after results |
| **Q2 Earnings** | Early July - Mid-July | Overlaps with stress test timing |
| **SCB Finalization** | August - September | Capital uncertainty |
| **Q3/Q4 Earnings** | Standard timing | Normal blackout windows |

**Key insight:** Banks have a **double blackout** in Q2 (stress test + earnings). This could amplify the effect.

### Bank Earnings Dates 2026

| Bank | Q1 2026 | Q2 2026 | Q3 2026 |
|------|---------|---------|---------|
| JPM | April 14 | ~July | ~October |
| GS | April 13 | ~July | ~October |
| MS | April 15 | July 15 | October 14 |
| BAC | April 15 | July 14 | October 14 |
| WFC | April 14 | July 14 | October 14 |
| C | April 14 | ~July | ~October |

**All banks report in the same 2-day window** (April 13-15, July 14-15, October 14). This is important for sector-wide effects.

### Banks Use Both 10b5-1 and Discretionary

Banks use a hybrid approach:
- **10b5-1 plans** for baseline repurchases (continues during blackouts)
- **Discretionary authority** for opportunistic buybacks (pauses during blackouts)

This means the blackout effect for banks may be **partial, not complete**.

## The 10b5-1 Problem

### Most Companies Can Continue Buying During Blackouts

| Finding | Implication |
|---------|-------------|
| ~25% of buyback announcements include 10b5-1 | Many programs have autopilot capability |
| ~60% of programs not affected by blackouts | Majority continue buying |
| Apple uses 10b5-1 | Our archetype may not fully "stop" during blackout |

### This Challenges Our Thesis

If buybacks continue via 10b5-1, the "demand removal" effect is weakened. The blackout may only affect the **discretionary portion** (~35-40% of buying), not the full program.

### Who Does NOT Use 10b5-1?

**Berkshire Hathaway** is the most prominent example of purely discretionary buybacks:
- No pre-set plan
- Warren Buffett decides based on intrinsic value
- Completely stops during blackouts (or when valuation not attractive)

**Question:** Are there other large buyback companies that are purely discretionary? These would theoretically show the strongest blackout effects.

## Revised Thesis

### What The Research Says

1. **Aggregate blackout effect is weak/non-existent** for most stocks
2. **10b5-1 plans** allow most programs to continue during blackouts
3. **Excess returns come from fundamentals**, not buyback mechanics

### What Might Still Work

1. **Intra-blackout timing** - Early blackout weakness, late blackout strength (our Apple finding)
2. **Regime effects** - Weak stocks entering blackout get hit harder
3. **Specific windows** - January (holiday liquidity), Q2 for banks (stress test overlap)
4. **Discretionary-heavy programs** - Companies that DON'T use 10b5-1

### Next Steps

1. **Backtest our specific approach** on banks and Visa:
   - Late blackout entry (not full period)
   - 15-day post-earnings exit
   - Focus on weak regime setups

2. **Find discretionary-only programs** - These should show strongest effects

3. **Test Q2 bank blackouts** - Double blackout (stress test + earnings) may amplify effect

4. **Compare to academic baseline** - Our edge may be in the TIMING, not the existence of the effect
